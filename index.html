What a patent actually is

A patent is the right to stop other people from making, using, or selling your invention for a set time.1 Think of it as a right to exclude, a fence around your idea. It does not hand you a product or a profit, and it does not give you the right to build the thing yourself.1 What it gives you is the legal power to keep copycats out.

Utility patentHow something works or is used. Lasts up to 20 years from filing.2
Design patentHow something looks. Lasts 15 years from grant.1
Plant patentA new, distinct plant reproduced without seeds. 20 years from filing.2

And they are not the same as a trademark or copyright. A patent protects an invention; a trademark protects a name or logo (also at the USPTO); a copyright protects creative work like writing or music (at the U.S. Copyright Office, automatically once it is fixed); a trade secret protects confidential information for as long as you keep it secret.5,6

Is it patentable? Four gates

The right kind of thing. A process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter. A bare idea or a law of nature is not patentable.4
Useful. It has a real, specific function.4
New. It isn't already in the prior art.4
Non-obvious. It isn't an obvious variation of what exists.4

Your first steps

Tap each step as you go.

  • ✓Write it down. Date it, sketch it, describe how it works.15
  • ✓Zip your lips before you file. The U.S. gives a one-year grace period after your own disclosure, but most countries give none, so file before you show.7
  • ✓Search first (free). USPTO Patent Public Search and Google Patents.8
  • ✓Consider a provisional. Locks an early date and “patent pending” for 12 months. It is never examined and is not a patent by itself.9
  • ✓Claim your discount. Most students qualify as a micro entity, about 80% off. A provisional is $65 at that rate.10,11,12
  • ✓Calendar the 12-month deadline to file the full application. This is where an attorney or agent earns their keep.9

Myths worth killing

Tap each one to bust it.

“You can patent an idea.”Tap
No. You patent a specific, described invention, not a bare concept.4
“I have to build a working model first.”Tap
No. The USPTO rarely requires a model. A clear written description is usually enough.1
“Mailing myself a letter protects me.”Tap
No. The “poor man's patent” has no legal effect. Since March 16, 2013 the U.S. is first-inventor-to-file, so only filing counts.15
“A provisional is a patent.”Tap
No. It's a 12-month placeholder that is never examined.9
“Patents are cheap and fast.”Tap
No. A full patent commonly costs thousands and often takes two or more years to grant.10
